\begin{abstract}
This project is one file. The source in the left pane, the editor you are
reading it in, and the engine that turned it into the PDF on the right are all
elements of the same document --- there is no server in the loop, and nothing
here was uploaded anywhere.
\end{abstract}

\tableofcontents

\section{Writing}

Type in the pane on the left and press \textbf{Recompile}, or leave
\emph{auto} on and it will typeset a moment after you stop. Words wrap and
paragraphs justify the way \LaTeX{} does them; a blank line starts a new one.

You get the usual marks: \textbf{bold}, \emph{emphasis}, \texttt{monospace},
quotes that curl like ``this'', dashes that are dashes---like that one---and
footnotes\footnote{Which collect at the foot of the page they were called
from, numbered in order.} where you want them.

\section{Mathematics}
\label{sec:maths}

Inline maths sits in the line: $e^{i\pi} + 1 = 0$, and $f : \R^n \to \R$ is
set in the maths italic you would expect.

Displayed maths gets its own space and, in an \texttt{equation}, its own
number:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:gauss}
  \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-x^2}\,dx = \sqrt{\pi}
\end{equation}

Equation~\ref{eq:gauss} is the one everybody knows. Alignment works the way
\texttt{amsmath} does it, on the ampersands:

\begin{align}
  (a+b)^2 &= a^2 + 2ab + b^2 \\
          &= \sum_{k=0}^{2} \binom{2}{k} a^{2-k} b^{k}
\end{align}

\[
  \frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = \alpha \nabla^2 u,
  \qquad
  A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{pmatrix}
\]
